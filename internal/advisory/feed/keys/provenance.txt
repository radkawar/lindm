Production advisory verification trust roots
============================================

These public OpenPGP certificates are verification material, not credentials.
They are embedded in the feed client and parsed at construction. Runtime feed
responses never add or replace trust roots. Full primary fingerprints are pinned
in signed.go, independently of configured mirror URLs. Root replacement,
rotation, expiry extension or newly published revocation requires a reviewed
update of this material. The maintained ProtonMail/go-crypto v1.5.2 v2 verifier
checks signatures, certified signing permissions, expiration and revocation in
these certificates; this is not a claim of live key-server revocation discovery.

Acquisition and freshness boundary
----------------------------------
Online individual CSAF sources require original payload, the published SHA256
record naming its exact basename, and a binary detached signature by the pinned
publisher. Changing distro/format cannot select an unrelated publisher's key.
HTTP without verified TLS, proof files above 64 KiB, individual JSON above
32 MiB, corrupt/missing proofs, invalid/revoked/expired keys and future signature
times are refused. SHA256 artifacts describe exact original vendor bytes;
signer fingerprints identify the actual authorized signing key.

Red Hat weekly source URLs identify the provider root. The client reads only
advisories/archive_latest.txt and vex/archive_latest.txt, each naming the strict
csaf_<distribution>_YYYY-MM-DD.tar.zst basename. It requires both snapshots;
requests do not crawl a directory or discover arbitrary URLs. Each compressed
archive is streamed to one private owned temporary file of at most 512 MiB,
verified before parsing, rewound for the parser, then closed and removed even
on errors or cancellation. Memory is not proportional to compressed archive
size. Two artifacts mean at most 1 GiB payload, two one-byte overflow probes,
and bounded pointers/proofs; eight logical requests, at most forty including
checked HTTPS redirects. Acquisition and parsing share a five-minute deadline,
with two-minute per-request HTTP timeouts. Decompression and record bounds
belong to the archive parser. The oldest valid detached-signature creation time
bounds source freshness: refetching an old signed archive cannot renew it.

The signed acquisition tests retain real Red Hat/SUSE payloads and signatures.
TLS orchestration tests serve those unchanged signed bytes under controlled
paths; JSON bytes transported under archive-shaped names test acquisition and
cleanup only, not a claim that those bytes are a vendor tar.zst archive. A
separate ephemeral key in the revocation policy test is not a vendor fixture or
a production trust root. No synthetic vendor record is used as evidence of
vendor advisory coverage.
